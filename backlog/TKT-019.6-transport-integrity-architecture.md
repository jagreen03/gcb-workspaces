# Objective

Design the **minimum transport contract** that makes [[TKT-019.5]]'s proven
failure modes either **impossible** (prevented structurally) or **explicitly
detectable** (flagged, correctly attributed, never silent) — without
implementing any of it. This is an architecture specification. No RTL
changes land in this ticket; [[TKT-019.5]]'s testbenches remain the
reproducible proof this design answers to, not something this ticket
re-runs or modifies.

**Layered integrity and epoch isolation, not heavyweight FEC** — the
Architect's explicit framing for this story. Every mechanism below adds
*detection* or *structural prevention* at or near zero payload-bandwidth
cost; none of them retransmits, corrects, or recovers payload content. That
is deliberate, not an oversight — see Deferred Mechanisms.

## Traceability — every mechanism closes a named requirement

[[TKT-019.5]] Section C extracted eight requirements from observed failures
(REQ-TX-ID-001/002, REQ-TX-INT-001/002, REQ-TX-FRM-001/002, REQ-TX-REC-001/002)
and Section D catalogued six candidate mitigations against them, selecting
none. This story's job is to **compose** a subset of those candidates (plus
one new policy-level synthesis — Protected Completion, below) into a single
coherent architecture that closes all eight, and to show the closure
explicitly, not just assert it.

## Mandatory mechanisms

### M1. Transaction / Epoch Identity

**Closes:** REQ-TX-ID-001 (partially — see M2 for the rest).
**Built from:** Section D #3 (sequence IDs / epoch counters).

Every transaction the mux emits carries an **epoch tag** — a small
(proposed: 4-8 bit, wrapping) counter incremented once per `start`. The tag
travels with *every word* of the transaction, not just the in-band header:
this is the load-bearing design choice. [[TKT-019.5]]'s Ghost Payload
mechanism worked precisely because the barrier's alignment condition
("every enabled lane non-empty") has no concept of *which transaction* a
lane's queued head word belongs to — a lane holding a stale epoch-N word
looks identical, to the barrier, to a lane holding a fresh epoch-N+1 word.

**Revised barrier invariant:** alignment requires every enabled lane
non-empty **and epoch-matched** — every contributing lane's head word must
carry the *same* epoch tag. A lane whose head word's epoch doesn't match
the others' is treated exactly like an empty lane for alignment purposes
(the barrier keeps waiting; `ALIGN_TIMEOUT` bounds that wait exactly as
today). This is a **structural prevention**, not a detection — a mixed-
epoch pop becomes impossible to form in the first place, independent of
whether M2's flush below runs in time.

Where the tag lives physically (an extension to `color_word_t`, a parallel
per-lane side-channel, or encoding inside currently-unused header bits) is
an implementation decision for the build story, not fixed here.

### M2. Hard Abort / Quarantine

**Closes:** REQ-TX-ID-002, REQ-TX-REC-001.
**Built from:** Section D #4 (watchdog-driven flush).

`txn_void` stops being a status flag a control plane may or may not act on
and becomes a **hard boundary**: the instant it fires, every enabled lane's
FIFO is flushed — its queued contents discarded — before any new
transaction may begin reusing those lanes. [[TKT-019.5]] Section A proved
the current RTL performs no flush of any kind; this is the direct fix for
that specific, demonstrated gap, and it is the **first line of defense** for
epoch contamination, with M1 as the backstop for whatever the flush timing
doesn't fully cover.

**Flush mechanism, specified at the contract level:** each lane's deskew
FIFO (`gcb_dwdm_async_skew_fifo` today) gains a `flush` input, read-domain-
only — on assertion, the read pointer jumps to the *already
read-domain-synchronised* copy of the write pointer the gray-code crossing
logic already maintains internally. This requires **no new CDC crossing** —
it reuses a value the async FIFO already has on the read side — and is
therefore safe to pulse the instant `txn_void` fires, with no additional
synchronisation delay. (This is the specific reason "flush," not "reset,"
is the right primitive: a full reset is a heavier, per-domain operation
with its own cross-domain assertion/de-assertion ordering concerns; a
read-side-only pointer-catch-up avoids all of that.)

**Quarantine window:** the interval between `txn_void` and flush
completion is bounded and knowable (the flush is a single-cycle pointer
move, not a multi-cycle drain) — the control plane contract is: do not
issue a new `start` until N cycles after `txn_void`, where N is specified
precisely once this is implemented and measured, not guessed here.

### M3. Explicit Length Consistency

**Closes:** REQ-TX-FRM-001 (the length-cross-check half).
**Built from:** Section D #5.

The receiver already decodes the declared length from the header before
doing anything else (today's RTL does this too). This mechanism makes that
decoded length **load-bearing**: the receiver computes the expected total
word count from it and compares that against the number of words actually
accumulated. [[TKT-019.5]] Section B's early-`is_tile_final` finding is
exactly the case this catches — 60 words accumulated against a length that
implies 65 were needed, a mismatch a word-count check catches regardless of
whether the termination flag agrees.

This mechanism alone **detects** (does not prevent) the early-termination
case; it does nothing for a payload-body or header bit flip that doesn't
change the word count. It is one input to M4, not a complete fix on its own
— stated explicitly so it is not mistaken for one.

### M4. Protected Completion

**Closes:** REQ-TX-FRM-001 (the other half — completes what M3 starts).
**Built from:** a policy-level synthesis of M3 + the CRC check (M5) + the
termination flag itself — no single one of the three is trusted alone.

This is the architectural answer to [[TKT-019.5]]'s core finding that a
*single bit* (`is_tile_final`) was the sole arbiter of "this transaction is
complete and good." The completion decision becomes a **three-way
agreement**, not a single signal:

1. the termination flag is observed on the expected lane, **and**
2. M3's word-count check confirms the accumulated count matches the
   length-implied expectation, **and**
3. M5's CRC check passes.

All three must hold before `payload_valid` may assert. Any one disagreeing
with the other two produces a **named, attributable fault** (not a silent
acceptance, and not a generic catch-all like today's `stuck`) — which of
the three disagreed is itself diagnostic information the control plane
receives, closing the "fault signal that misattributes the cause" pattern
[[TKT-019.5]] Section B found in every corrupted-completion case it tested.

### M5. CRC / Integrity Check

**Closes:** REQ-TX-INT-001, REQ-TX-INT-002.
**Built from:** Section D #1.

A checksum (proposed: CRC-16, hardware-cheap, adequate single/few-bit-flip
detection coverage for a transport this size; the exact polynomial is an
implementation decision) computed over the framed payload **and** header at
the mux, carried as a trailer in the wire format, recomputed and compared
at the demux. A mismatch produces a distinct `integrity_fail` output — never
a silent `payload_valid` with wrong bytes underneath, which is what
[[TKT-019.5]] Section B found in every payload-body and header-field
corruption case tested.

**Explicitly not claimed:** CRC-16 does not guarantee detection of every
possible multi-bit corruption pattern — it is a cheap, well-characterised
*detector* for the single/few-bit-flip class this story's adversarial tests
actually exercised, not a cryptographic integrity guarantee. Sizing it
against a real bit-error distribution is optical-channel territory (see
Deferred Mechanisms) and is not claimed here.

### M6. Watchdog Timer

**Closes:** REQ-TX-FRM-002, REQ-TX-REC-002.
**Built from:** Section D #6.

A receiver-local timer, counting from a transaction's first accumulated
word, distinct from the CDC barrier's own `ALIGN_TIMEOUT` (which governs
lane *arrival*, not reassembly *completion*). If M4's three-way agreement
hasn't resolved within the watchdog's bound, the receiver forces itself
back to a clean, reusable state and asserts a **distinctly-named**
`reassembly_timeout` fault — not the CDC's `stuck`/`txn_void` vocabulary,
which [[TKT-019.5]] Section B found gets asserted for an unrelated reason
(all lanes' FIFOs draining with nothing left to wait for) in exactly this
scenario today, actively misleading whoever reads it. This mechanism is
also what gives REQ-TX-REC-002 its bounded recovery path — the receiver's
internal accumulation state (the `word_base`-equivalent) is guaranteed to
return to zero within the watchdog's bound, closing the "wedges future
transactions" risk [[TKT-019.4]] and [[TKT-019.5]] both flagged.

## Requirement closure — stated explicitly, not asserted

| requirement | closed by |
|---|---|
| REQ-TX-ID-001 | M1 (structural prevention) |
| REQ-TX-ID-002 | M1 (backstop) + M2 (primary) |
| REQ-TX-INT-001 | M5 |
| REQ-TX-INT-002 | M5 |
| REQ-TX-FRM-001 | M3 + M4 (jointly — neither alone is sufficient) |
| REQ-TX-FRM-002 | M6 |
| REQ-TX-REC-001 | M2 |
| REQ-TX-REC-002 | M6 |

All eight close. No requirement is closed by more mechanisms than
necessary, and none is left partially addressed without saying so (ID-002
is explicitly two-layered — M1 as backstop, M2 as the primary fix — and
FRM-001 is explicitly joint, not satisfied by either M3 or M4 alone).

## Deferred mechanisms

**FEC and retry are explicitly deferred**, not rejected. Both would commit
to an error *rate* and *pattern* this project does not yet have a measured
or modelled basis for — that is physical-channel territory
([[GCB-037]]'s link budget exists, but a BER/error-pattern model for this
specific transport's optical channel does not, and the [[TKT-019.4]]
epistemic boundary applies here exactly as it did there). Every mechanism
above is a **detector or a structural preventer**, not a corrector — this
story closes the gap between "silently wrong" and "loudly, correctly
wrong," and stops there deliberately. Designing FEC or retry before that
error model exists would be sizing a fix against a guess, which is the
exact failure mode the Orange-Team methodology exists to prevent.

## AI Orchestration Priorities (PBG Evaluation) — for execution, not graded now

Per Task 5: **no PBG grade is assigned on drafting.** `telemetry_color:
pending`, no `telemetry:` block, no `pbg_o` flag — consistent with every
prior TKT-019.x draft. The grade, if any, is earned when a build story
implements this specification against real RTL and cocotb evidence — this
ticket is architecture-only and may itself close without a grade if the
Architect treats it the way [[TKT-019.5]]'s audit was treated, depending on
how the next story is scoped.

* **Orange (methodology, not a grade):** every mechanism above traces to a
  named REQ-TX-* requirement, which itself traces to a specific observed
  [[TKT-019.4]]/[[TKT-019.5]] failure — nothing here is motivated by what
  would be convenient to build.
* **Green (target, at a future build story):** all eight requirements
  closed against real RTL, verified with cocotb tests that specifically
  re-run [[TKT-019.5]]'s adversarial scenarios (Ghost Payload, the five
  corruption categories) and show each one now produces the correctly-
  attributed, named fault this architecture specifies instead of silent
  acceptance or a hang.
* **Yellow (reject):** implementing M5 (CRC) alone and declaring the
  transport "integrity-protected" while leaving M1/M2 (epoch isolation)
  unaddressed — Ghost Payload is not a content-integrity problem and a CRC
  over correct-but-misattributed data would pass.
* **Red (reject):** implementing FEC or retry in the next build story
  without the error-model justification this ticket explicitly defers;
  silently narrowing M4's three-way agreement to fewer than three checks
  without saying so.
* **Purple (possible, not claimed):** if M1's epoch-tag mechanism proves
  cheap enough, it may generalise to other shared-resource reuse points in
  this fabric beyond this specific transport — not claimed or scoped here.
